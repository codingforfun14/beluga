# 16 — Hook per short: motion-designed hooks for vertical video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)

Research date: 22 Oct 2026. Scope: references, a technique taxonomy, and retention data. Media was not downloaded.

How to read this: I could not watch any video frame by frame. The 0-3 s descriptions for vidIQ items come from vidIQ's machine-generated `hook_0_3s` fields (visual / text / audio), which I paraphrased. Frame and second timings marked "(est.)" are my estimates at 30 fps, based on the technique and the source text. They are not measurements. **URL status:** "opened" means I fetched the URL or the tool returned it. "unverified" means I built the URL from an ID or recalled it, and never opened it.

## Which brands, creators and studios are known for excellent motion-graphic hooks?

### Takeaway
The best-documented brand cases are **Duolingo** (mascot + trend audio + reveal, run in-house), **Spotify Wrapped** (a data-driven, templated motion system built in Rive), **Instagram's own motion identity** (Studio Dumbar/DEPT®), and **ManvsMachine for Squarespace** (internet-UI motion language). On a 30-day vidIQ outlier scan (posted after 1 Oct 2025), most over-performing motion hooks were creator or tool-demo content, not brand ads. They open on the **finished visual result in frame 1**, use **3D or kinetic type with SFX from frame 0**, or use a **split screen with a talking head**. I found **no public frame-level case studies** for Notion, Linear, Apple, Ryanair or Liquid Death. Those entries below are account-level pointers, marked unverified.

#### B. Taxonomy of reusable hook techniques (15)

Timings are practical estimates (30 fps) anchored to the platform figures in the retention section: 50% of TikTok ad impact lands in the first 2 s, the proposition should come within 3 s, and the hook within 6 s.

1. **Result-first / payoff preview** — show the finished output or transformation on frame 1, then explain how.
   - When to use: tutorials, SaaS features, design and tool demos.
   - Timing: the payoff is visible at f0, the cut to the process at 1-2 s.
   - References: #12, #15, #18, #20.
2. **Text slam** (one or two oversized words punching in on the beat) — big type scaling from 120% to 100% with a hard ease-out over about 4-6 frames.
   - When to use: bold claims, sound-off feeds.
   - Timing: on screen by f0-6; hold at least 0.5 s per word.
   - References: #24 ("YOU"), #26, #16.
3. **Kinetic typography as object** — letters behave physically (drop, assemble, stack) and enact the word's meaning.
   - When to use: brand and type showcases, looping content.
   - Timing: motion from f0; the word becomes readable by about 2-3 s.
   - References: #13, #14.
4. **Object reveal / assembly** (exploded view, build-up) — components fly in and assemble.
   - When to use: product and hardware launches, logo stings.
   - Timing: start mid-motion at f0 (no slow build); the hero form resolves by about 2-3 s.
   - References: #21, #25.
5. **Context-then-reveal / pan reveal** — open on a normal scene, then pan or cut to a surprising element.
   - When to use: mascots, comedy, brand characters.
   - Timing: the reveal at 1-2 s.
   - References: #1, #4.
6. **Hard-cut pattern interrupt** — a static or mundane first beat broken by a sudden cut, SFX or gesture (door slam, punch).
   - When to use: any feed where attention drifts.
   - Timing: the interrupt by f10-20 (about 0.3-0.7 s).
   - References: #19, #23, #24.
7. **Split screen (demo + face)** — the top half shows the visual, the bottom half the talking head.
   - When to use: reviews, education, reaction.
   - Timing: both halves active from f0.
   - References: #16, #17, #20. Vidmob/TikTok data backs the face half (see the retention section).
8. **"Wait for it" / physics anticipation** — a set-up that makes viewers predict an outcome (marble runs, races, "which one wins").
   - When to use: entertainment, looping, comment bait.
   - Timing: the set-up readable in under 1 s; the payoff delayed to keep watch time.
   - References: #22.
9. **Question / curiosity line on screen** — a "secret", a "how does X work", an open loop.
   - When to use: explainers.
   - Timing: the question readable within 1-1.5 s (TikTok's 5-10 words per second guideline). Meta names the "question/invitation" hook as one of its three types.
   - References: #18, #23; [Meta via SMT](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182).
10. **Value promise / statement of intent** — state the benefit or what viewers will see (Meta's other two hook types).
    - When to use: performance ads, how-tos.
    - Timing: within 3 s (TikTok's guidance on stating the proposition).
    - References: #12 ("spice up your videos"), #18.
11. **Trend / meme template remix** (and stitch/duet) — borrow a known format.
    - When to use: brand personality, reactive content.
    - Evidence: TikTok says remixing a trend lifts watch time 14% ([SMT 2023](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-new-notes-maximize-ad-effectiveness/694989/)).
    - References: #2, #3.
12. **POV / relatable caption** — a "POV:" or "when you..." line over footage.
    - When to use: lifestyle, B2B humour.
    - References: #1, #19.
13. **Personal data reveal / stat slam** — a big number or personalised stat animates in.
    - When to use: recaps, year-in-review, fintech, fitness.
    - References: #5 (Spotify Wrapped).
14. **Native-gesture transition** (swipe, scroll or UI interaction as the move into the body) — motion that mimics platform behaviour.
    - When to use: app brands, UI-heavy products.
    - References: #6 (Instagram motion system), #7 (ManvsMachine UI props), #12.

15. **Before/after transition** — show the "before" state labelled on frame 1, then flip to the "after" with a cut, wipe or grade switch.
    - When to use: transformations (grading, design, product results), humour.
    - Timing: the "before" readable at f0 with a label; the switch at about 1-2 s; often looped.
    - References: #28, #29. vidIQ also surfaced several non-motion before/after outliers (e.g. a 48.2M-view @antoniobrown clip with a "BEFORE" label, https://www.tiktok.com/@antoniobrown/video/7688384853187890446). The format was the most common template returned for the before/after query.

Not observed in either vidIQ query: **progress bar** and **zoom punch-in**. Both queries returned before/after templates instead. These are common editing devices, but I have no cited reference for them, so treat them as uncited.

### Inferences
- In 2025-26 outliers, the dominant motion hook is "show the finished thing at frame 0". Almost none of the high scorers use a logo intro, which matches Vidmob's finding that an early logo cuts 6-second view-through by 14%.
- Sound starts at frame 0 in every vidIQ outlier (a beat drop, SFX or speech). The audio cue lands on the same frame as the first motion.
- Physical, satisfying 3D (blocks, marble runs, exploded views, zachdfilms-style explainers) looks like a strong 2025-26 motion-hook genre. It loops naturally, which helps completion.

## What do TikTok, Meta and YouTube guidance say about hooks?

### Takeaway
TikTok: put the hook in the first 6 s, state the proposition in the first 3 s, and pace text at 5-10 words per second. TikTok also says 50% of ad impact comes in the first 2 s. Meta: "nail the hook within the first few seconds"; it names three hook types and says over 80% of Reels are played with sound on. YouTube does not publish an official hook-timing rule. It exposes a "viewed vs swiped away" metric and allows Shorts of up to 3 minutes (since Oct 2024).

### Cited Findings
- **TikTok:**
  - "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds"
  - "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness"
  - "5-10 words per second when using text"
  - 9:16 vertical, at least 720p, key content inside the UI safe zone
  - Sound or music listed as a basic
  - No length recommendation on that page.
  - Source: [TikTok Ads Help — Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)
- **TikTok:** "50% of the impact from a TikTok ad is realized in the first 2 seconds". The first 6 s deliver about 90% of the ad-recall impact and about 80% of the awareness impact. Creative storytelling lifted watch time 16%, remixing a trend 14%, and real people 9% — [Social Media Today, 27 Sep 2023](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-new-notes-maximize-ad-effectiveness/694989/)
- **Vidmob × TikTok "Science of the Hook"** (1,678 ads, 7.3B impressions, ads run 1 Jan-15 Oct 2023):
  - Ads that hook viewers lift engagement 2x and purchase intent 43%.
  - Everyday people or creators are 1.7x more likely to hook than celebrities; celebrities cut 6-second view-through by 13%.
  - Direct-to-camera footage lifts 2-second view-through 14% and hooking power 50%.
  - A standalone product shot lifts 6-second view-through 17%; an **early logo cuts it 14%**.
  - Making the brand the narrative hero gives 1.5x hooking power.
  - Caveat: a TikTok partner study.
  - Source: [Vidmob](https://vidmob.com/resource/tiktok-hook-analysis)
- **Meta** (Dec 2025):
  - "Great Reels nail the hook within the first few seconds."
  - Three hook types: value promise, statement of intent, question/invitation.
  - A/B test your hooks.
  - Younger audiences consume content at 3x the speed of older viewers.
  - Music or voiceover gives up to 13% higher incremental conversions.
  - Source: [Social Media Today, 17 Dec 2025](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182)
- **Meta × Toluna Reels study** (100 ads × 100 participants; DE, UK, IT, TR; research in 2024, published Dec 2025). Each figure is the likelihood of ranking in the top 20%:
  - A hook using visual and audio cues in the first 1-2 s: 1.5x purchase intent
  - Brand and main message in the first 5 s: 1.7x
  - Emojis as a native visual element: 2.5x (direct response)
  - Product context: 5.3x
  - Brand presence capped at 25% of duration: 4.8x
  - Speech + music: 2.0x brand interest
  - Meta: over 80% of Reels are played with sound on.
  - Source: [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-and-toluna-study-finds-emojis-lift-reels-purchase-intent-2-5x/)
- **YouTube:** Shorts of up to 3 minutes allowed from 15 Oct 2024 — [PetaPixel, 4 Oct 2024](https://petapixel.com/2024/10/04/youtube-shorts-expands-video-length-to-three-minutes/) (search snippet; not opened)
- **YouTube:** Public Shorts view counts changed in March 2025 to count starts and replays with no minimum watch time. YouTube publishes no universal "good retention" threshold. "Viewed vs swiped away" is the share who watched rather than swiped — [Bytecap, Jul 2026](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- The platform guidance converges on a hook by 1-2 s, the proposition or brand by 3-5 s, and attention-holding through 6 s. For motion work, that means the first meaningful frame and the audio cue at f0-f15, and readable type by about f30-45.
- Sound-on matters more on Reels (80%+), but captions remain the safety net.

## Retention data, ideal lengths and captions — and which techniques are trending in 2025-26?

### Takeaway
Hard retention benchmarks are scarce and mostly come from third parties. Commonly cited targets for YouTube Shorts are 75-80%+ "viewed" and about 80% average percentage viewed, but these are unsourced. TikTok's own text-pacing rule (5-10 words per second) is the only official caption figure found. The 2025-26 trends seen in outliers are result-first demos, split-screen creator + demo, satisfying 3D or physics loops, kinetic type as object, and AI motion-tool showcases.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Shorts targets: "viewed vs swiped away" at least 75-80%, average percentage viewed about 80% or more (e.g. 40 s on a 50 s Short). These are attributed to unnamed "strategists and creators"; treat them as guidance, not platform data — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-viral)
- Bytecap suggests comparing Shorts in length cohorts (15-30 s, 30-60 s, longer) instead of using one ideal length — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)
- TikTok: longer ads reduce audience size; shorter, native creative is suggested — [SMT 2023](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-new-notes-maximize-ad-effectiveness/694989/)
- Captions and text: TikTok recommends 5-10 words per second and captions or overlays for context — [TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en). Meta/Toluna found native emojis lift DR purchase intent 2.5x — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-and-toluna-study-finds-emojis-lift-reels-purchase-intent-2-5x/)
- Lengths in the outlier sample (TikTok): 13 s (looped 3D type), 25 s, 27 s, 32 s, 38 s, 61 s — vidIQ outlier search (see the catalogue)
- Trend signals 2025-26: AI-generated motion-graphic tools as a content topic (#12, #17, #18, #16) and a "trending SaaS text animation" style (#15) — vidIQ outliers. Trend-roundup articles exist ([Graphic Design Junction, Jan 2026](https://graphicdesignjunction.com/2026/01/video-and-motion-creative-trends-2026/); [Tangence](https://www.tangence.com/blog/?p=1093)) but were not opened.

### Inferences
- A practical spec for a motion hook: first motion and audio at f0 · hook resolved by 2 s · proposition by 3 s · brand by 5 s with presence of 25% or less · text at 10 words per second or fewer, inside the UI safe zone · loopable ending for videos under 15 s.
- The best-performing motion hooks in 2025-26 reward craft visible in frame 1 (3D, kinetic type) or the face + demo combination. A pure logo sting performs poorly.
